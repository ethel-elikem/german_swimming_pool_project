## Task B — German vs. non-German split

Inserted after B9 in the same notebook (`## Task B` section, B1–B5, cells 69–79). Note: cell 21 already has an unrelated `### B1 — Prevalence` section from the original notebook — this reuses the "B1" label for a different subsection; consider renaming to avoid confusion.

- **B1 gate: PASS.** Median `n_rac_nongerman` = 10 (≥5); zero-numerator share 4.0% (German) and 0.0% (non-German), both under 1/3.
- **⚠ Root cause of the gate's zero-numerator asymmetry — a real classifier bug, not noise.** The non-German group has *more* volume than German (mean `n_soc_nongerman`=887 vs `n_soc_german`=668), which looks backwards for reviews of German pools. Traced to source: **"United States" is the prediction tool's second-largest category overall** (124,521 of ~1.27M rows, behind only Germany's 354,465 — more than 7x Türkiye's 17,544). Since `to_group5` folds anything outside {Germany, Arab, Türkiye, Unknown} into "Other," and Task B defines non-German = Arab+Turkish+Other, all of that "United States" volume lands in the non-German bucket. Spot-checked 30 "United States"-predicted names at random: overwhelmingly German (*Angela Schmidt, Andreas Beutke, Sonja Porske, Gorden Heuer, Silvia Bremhorst, Michael Hammel*), not American. Quantified: **22.9% of "Other"-predicted racialised reviews (899 of 3,929) are specifically "United States"-predicted** — i.e., roughly **19% of the entire non-German racialised-review count (899 of 4,762)** is very likely misclassified Germans, not genuine non-Germans.
  **Implication:** this contamination biases Task B's group-specific comparison *toward* finding no difference (mislabeled Germans in the non-German bucket make that group behave more like the German group). B3's headline "no significant difference" (p=0.133) should therefore be read as **"no difference, or a real difference attenuated by ~19% mislabeled Germans in the comparison group"** — not as clean evidence of no difference. This is a more serious and more concrete finding than anything anticipated in the original Task D brief (which expected Turkish/Arab names absorbed into German/Unknown, not the reverse).
- **B2:** four NB-GLM fits (shared vs. group-specific denominator × German/non-German) — all converge cleanly, rate ratios in `outputs/BSPLIT2_*.csv`.
- **B3 (headline test):** stacked interaction model, group-specific denominator, cluster SEs by constituency. Joint Wald test on all 5 interaction terms: **χ²=8.45, df=5, p=0.133** — predictor slopes do **not** differ significantly between German and non-German reviewers. Moran's I significant for both groups' residuals (German I=0.082 p=0.012; non-German I=0.094 p=0.008) — real, separate spatial structure per group, the other predicted piece of directionality evidence.
- **B4 sensitivity:** folding Unknown into non-German (p=0.128) and treating it as a third level (p=0.374) both agree with the headline (non-significant) — **conclusion survives.**
- **B5:** the shared-denominator version of the same stacked model *does* show significant interactions (joint p≈0.000) — exactly the composition confound the brief predicted (reviewer-pool ethnic mix is itself spatially structured), and a useful internal check that the group-specific version is doing its job.

**Unresolved:** none for the modelling itself. The directionality question (do German and non-German reviewers racialise differently in response to the same context) reads as **no** on this data, contingent on the ethnicity classifier being reasonably accurate — see Task D.

## Task C — Rate total (new notebook: `rate_total_analysis.ipynb`)

Standalone notebook, does not touch `supervisor_run_2026-08-16.ipynb`.

- **C1:** `rate_total` mean 0.0063 vs. `rate_social` mean 0.0158 — systematically lower, as expected (`total_text_reviews ≥ social_reviews` always). **Scatter of the two rates across all 299 constituencies: Pearson r=0.984, Spearman ρ=0.980** (`outputs/rate_total/C1_rate_social_vs_rate_total_scatter.png`) — reassuringly high, and reconciles neatly with the C7 finding below: `share_social` being statistically "patterned" doesn't stop the two outcomes from tracking each other almost 1:1 at the constituency level, so that composition effect is real but small in practice.
- **C3 model selection, rerun from scratch:** the correlation-redundancy screen (Step 1–2) is mathematically DV-independent, so it necessarily reproduces the rate_social drops (`unemployment_total_pct`, `second_vote_greens_pct`). The LOWESS shape check (Step 3) was automated as a quantitative proxy (slope-sign agreement + linear R² between West/East) rather than eyeballed — flagged as a methodological substitution; the saved panel (`outputs/rate_total/C3b_lowess_shape_panels.png`) is worth a visual sanity check. Fit-then-prune-by-significance (Step 4–5) leaves **only `foreign_population_pct_c`** surviving for `rate_total` — every other rate_social predictor (`log_population_density_c`, `sgb_ii_recipients_per_1k_c`, `is_east`, `in_split_city`) drops out non-significant.
  **→ A genuinely different predictor set survives for `rate_total`.** Do not assume rate_social's winners transfer.
- **C3d — fair-comparison fit:** the rate_social/rate_total coefficient comparison (Figure 1) originally compared a 5-predictor model against a 1-predictor one, which isn't a like-for-like comparison. Refit `rate_total` on rate_social's exact 5-predictor set (not the pruned selection) as a second, explicitly-labelled model: all 5 predictors now have the **same sign** and **overlapping CIs** as rate_social. Notably, this full 5-predictor fit has *lower* AIC (2287.8) than the significance-pruned "selected" model (2300.8) — the prune-by-p-value procedure (inherited unchanged from the rate_social method) landed on a worse-fitting model here. Both the selected (1-predictor) and full-5-predictor fits are now in the C8 comparison table, clearly labelled, so neither the selection result nor the fair comparison is lost.
- **C5 diagnostics:** dispersion 0.961 (well-specified NB fit); Moran's I on residuals = 0.274 (p=0.001) — noticeably *more* spatially clustered than rate_social's final-model residuals, worth flagging as an open question (denominator choice changes residual spatial structure, not just point estimates).
- **C6:** predicted `rate_total` at p25 vs. p75 of `foreign_population_pct`: 0.0048 → 0.0071.
- **C7 bridge model:** `share_social` is **patterned** (`foreign_population_pct_c` significant, p=0.033; 1/5 predictors overall). Conclusion: **`rate_total` effects are ambiguous between "more racialising" and "more social reviewing"** — the two outcomes are not interchangeable, contra the "presentational only" null hypothesis.
- **C7b/C7c — additivity check (mechanism, not assertion):** refit `share_social` as an NB count model with `offset=log(total_text_reviews)` (same family/link as the other models, so coefficients are on a comparable log scale), then checked β_social + β_share against β_total (using the C3d full-5-predictor fit) for each of the 5 predictors. Max absolute deviation across all 5: **0.024** (log scale) — additivity holds cleanly. This numerically demonstrates `rate_total = rate_social × share_social` is recovered by the three independently-fit models, not just true by definition of the raw counts.
- **C8:** with the full-5-predictor fit, all 5 predictors are now comparable across rate_social and rate_total (same sign, CIs overlap for every one) — a much stronger comparison than the 1-predictor selected model allowed. The selected model's coefficient is also retained in the table as a separate labelled column. AIC/dispersion table shows `share_social`'s naive binomial dispersion is very high (42.5) — HC1 robust SEs mitigate this, but the binomial variance assumption is clearly wrong for that model; treat its p-value, not its raw SEs, with some care.

**Unresolved:** the LOWESS-by-eye substitution should ideally get a human look at the saved panel before treating the Step-3 shape calls as final.

- **⚠ Real classifier bug found without needing the human-coded ground truth: "United States" over-prediction.** No formal precision/recall validation has been run yet (needs the researcher's hand-coding — nothing has been coded, 0/599). But a targeted spot-check, prompted by an anomaly in the B1 gate numbers, already surfaced a concrete, high-confidence bug: the prediction tool assigns **"United States" to 124,521 reviewers** (its 2nd-largest category, ahead of Türkiye's 17,544 by 7x), and a random sample of 30 such names were overwhelmingly, unambiguously German (*Angela Schmidt, Andreas Beutke, Sonja Porske, Gorden Heuer, Silvia Bremhorst*). This single bug explains ~19% of the entire non-German racialised-review count used in Task B (see Task B section above) and should be treated as confirmed, not merely suspected — it doesn't need Cohen's kappa to believe it. **Recommendation:** before relying further on the German/non-German split, consider re-running Task B with "United States"-predicted reviewers either excluded or reassigned to Unknown, as a robustness check against the current result.
